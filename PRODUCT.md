# donna.fyi — Product Context

**Register:** brand

## Product Purpose

donna.fyi is the public face of **Donna** — a self-hosted AI chief of staff that runs on the Hermes Agent runtime (Nous Research), MiniMax M2.7 for reasoning, and Honcho for semantic memory. The site is not a product page or a sales tool. It exists to make Donna — and by extension, the person who built and runs her — legible to three audiences simultaneously: AI / agent builders, recruiters / hiring managers, and friends Zach DMs the link to.

The redesign moved the site from "hah I have this and you don't" personal-brand flex to a serious technical artifact that happens to be written in Donna's voice.

## Users

Three audiences, all wanting the same thing — *evidence of substance, delivered with personality*:

1. **AI / agent-space builders & open-source folk** — the Nous Research / Hermes / Honcho crowd. They click the outbound links. They evaluate the stack story. They scroll the diagram.
2. **Recruiters / hiring managers** — landed from Zach's GitHub or a CV link. They use Donna as evidence of how Zach thinks, builds, and operates end-to-end. They care about the system being real, not Donna herself.
3. **People Zach sends the link to** — friends, peers, conversation handoffs. They want it to still feel like Donna.

Not in scope as users: marketing leads, sales pipeline, the general public. There is no CTA, no signup, no conversion funnel.

## Brand

**Donna Paulsen from Suits.** The persona is named after her. The voice is confident, sharp, slightly amused, occasionally cheeky. Easter eggs throughout the site reference Suits characters and Donna's catchphrases. She doesn't ask, she has already done it. She doesn't sugarcoat. She remembers everything.

**Two authors live in the brand:** Donna (violet accent) and Zach (amber accent). The blog will eventually carry posts from both, visibly differentiated.

## Tone

- Direct. Declarative sentences.
- Cheeky in the personality moments (hero, easter eggs, footer), neutral-factual in the dossier (How She Works, Memory, Skill surface).
- No corporate-speak, no marketing copy, no "we believe" mission-statement language.
- Specific names over generic categories. "Hermes Agent" not "an agent framework." "MiniMax M2.7" not "a long-context LLM."
- No em dashes (the skill bans these and Donna agrees).

## Anti-references

The site is **not**:

- A SaaS landing page (no "Sign up" / "Book a demo" / "Free trial")
- A portfolio page with project cards
- A personal blog homepage with photos and a "Hi, I'm Zach" intro
- A documentation site (it's an artifact about a system, not the system's docs)
- A funnel for anything

It must avoid feeling like any of the following:

- The default Vercel / Linear cold-blue dark-tech aesthetic — too generic
- A typical AI startup landing page (gradient hero + 3-up feature grid + testimonial carousel + CTA)
- A Webflow template
- Anything with "Built with v0" energy

## Strategic principles

1. **Substance over swagger.** The technical claims are real, sourced from real tech (Hermes, M2.7, Honcho), and link out to canonical pages. Personality lives in voice; substance carries the page.
2. **Voice opens and closes, dossier dominates the middle.** Three acts. The seam between them is intentional and reinforced by typography, motion, and density.
3. **No personal-deployment specifics.** No host names, no message counts, no internal paths. The site talks about the architecture pattern, not Zach's specific instance.
4. **Two distinct authors.** Donna writes mostly. Zach writes occasionally. Their visual identities are different.
5. **One-time read, occasional return.** Most visitors come once. Reward that visit with density and craft. The blog is the return loop.

## Success criteria

A first-time visitor — from any of the three audiences — leaves the page able to articulate:

1. What Donna actually is (an agent, not a chatbot; self-hosted; specific stack)
2. How she's built (Hermes + M2.7 + Honcho, with the architecture diagram visible enough to be mentally referenceable)
3. That whoever made her takes craft seriously
